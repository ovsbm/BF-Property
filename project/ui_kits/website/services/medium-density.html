What it is

Where multi-unit work tips into a small estate.

Once a site supports five or more units, the work changes. There is more consenting, more services design, a body corporate to stand up, and usually a titled-units strategy from the beginning rather than as an afterthought.

BF Property has run medium-density developments through the Christchurch Replacement Plan years and now under the National Policy Statement on Urban Development. The consent path is well-understood and the build economics are settled.

Typical configurations: six-unit terrace, eight-unit pair-of-rows, twelve-unit mixed-typology block.

What is included

What changes from a multi-unit job.

  1. Phase 01

    Yield optimisation

    How many units the site can carry under current zoning, with what consenting risk.

  2. Phase 02

    Resource consent

    Discretionary or non-complying activity. Pre-app, urban design panel where required.

  3. Phase 03

    Services and civils

    Stormwater, wastewater capacity, vehicle crossings, driveway sealing, retaining.

  4. Phase 04

    Staged build

    Programme broken into stages so cashflow tracks with completed units.

  5. Phase 05

    Body corp setup

    Titles lodged, body corp rules drafted, AGM 1 held before final handover.

  6. Phase 06

    Handover

    Per unit, with practical completion notices going to your nominated property manager.

When this fits

Best fits for medium-density.

This fits if

  • Consolidated site of 800 m² plus, or two adjacent titles you can amalgamate.
  • Capital sized for a multi-million-dollar build.
  • You want titles to retain long-term or to sell in stages.
  • You have a sensible exit strategy.

This is not right if

  • Site under 600 m². See Multi-unit and townhouses.
  • Mixed-use commercial-residential. Outside our typology.
  • Build-to-rent at high density (twenty units plus). Better with a different builder.
