Subdivide then sell, or subdivide then build?

A worked example on a typical Halswell site. The numbers usually favour building. Here is why, and the conditions under which the answer flips.

The site

Hypothetical: a 700 m² Halswell residential lot, currently one 1960s weatherboard house. Owner inherited the property. Wants to capitalise but is open to advice on how.

Path one: subdivide then sell

Subdivision into three lots: roughly $90k in consenting, survey, and services. Sale price of three vacant lots in Halswell sits at $310k to $340k each. Net to owner: roughly $850k to $930k after agent fees and tax.

Path two: subdivide and build

BF Property runs subdivision plus three-unit build. All-in cost roughly $1.55m. Completed value of three three-bedroom units in Halswell: $2.55m to $2.7m. Net to owner if sold: $900k to $1.05m. If retained: gross yield 4.9 percent on $2.6m portfolio.

Which path?

Build wins if the owner has access to capital (or staged finance), has 14 months of patience, and either wants to retain for yield or believes the completed-unit market is steadier than the bare-section market. Sell wins if the owner needs cash immediately or wants nothing more to do with the site.

When the answer flips

Sell can win on small sites (under 600 m²), on sites with high consenting risk, or where the owner's capital is fully committed elsewhere. We have written assessments saying sell more often than people expect us to.
